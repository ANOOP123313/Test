Origin Story

How It All
Unfolded

2020 — The Spark

Falling in Love with the Screen

It started with a YouTube thumbnail. I was twelve minutes into a rabbit hole of video editing tutorials when I noticed that the really good channels all had one thing in common — their visuals just felt right. Clean, striking, purposeful. That feeling hooked me before I even had a name for it. I downloaded Photoshop with a free trial I'd refresh every month, and spent late nights on nothing but text effects and colour grading. No roadmap, no mentor — just curiosity and caffeine.

2021 — First Real Work

Freelancing Before I Knew the Word

A friend asked me to design a poster for their college event. Then another friend. Then someone they knew. Before I realised it, I was a freelance graphic designer — completely by accident. I was designing social media creatives, profile pictures and event banners for people across Kerala. Every project taught me something the tutorials couldn't: that real clients have real feelings about their brand, and your job is to translate those feelings into visuals they didn't know they needed.

2022–2023 — Deepening the Craft

250+ Clients and Counting

Over two years, I worked with over 250 clients on graphic design projects — from student content creators to small businesses looking for a professional face. I learned Adobe Illustrator, moved into Lightroom for photo editing, and started using Inkscape and GIMP alongside the Adobe suite. More importantly, I learned that speed without quality is noise, and that a designer who listens is always better than one who just delivers. My satisfaction rate held strong at 4.8/5 — and those reviews meant more to me than any certificate.

2023 — The Pivot

Discovering UI/UX — Design With a Purpose

I stumbled onto Figma one evening and spent the next six hours inside it without blinking. Something clicked. Graphic design had always been about how things look — but UI/UX was about how things work. It asked deeper questions: Why would a user tap this? What happens if they're confused here? How do we reduce friction at every step? I was obsessed. I started rebuilding apps I used daily — just to practice, just to understand the thinking behind every decision.

2024 — Building a New Skillset

From Figma Mockups to Real Prototypes

My first year in UI/UX design was intense and deliberate. I learned wireframing from scratch, built interactive prototypes in Figma, taught myself Framer for motion-driven web builds, and picked up enough HTML and CSS to bridge the gap between design and code. I designed a personal finance app, a SaaS analytics dashboard, a meal planning app, and more — each one a new lesson in information architecture, micro-interactions, and design systems. I began collaborating with developers, learning their language, and understanding what it actually takes to ship clean design at scale.

2025–Now — What's Next

Stepping Into the Industry as a Fresher

I'm a fresher — and I say that with pride. I've spent years building real skills on real projects before ever stepping into a studio. I come with a portfolio shaped by hundreds of genuine client interactions, a mindset trained on both aesthetics and usability, and an energy that only comes from genuinely loving the work. I'm not looking for my first project — I'm looking for the right team to grow alongside. If you're building something meaningful, let's talk.

Design Philosophy

How I Think
About Design

🎯
PURPOSE FIRST

Every design decision needs a reason. Beauty is empty without function, and function without beauty goes unnoticed. I try to find the intersection where both coexist without compromise.

👁
EMPATHY IN PIXELS

Good design starts with understanding people — their frustrations, habits, and expectations. I design for the person who doesn't read instructions, because most people don't.

⚡
CLARITY OVER CLEVERNESS

A clever interface that confuses is a failed interface. I chase simplicity — not because it's easy, but because stripping everything down to only what matters is the hardest thing to do well.
